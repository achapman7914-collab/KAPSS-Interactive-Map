What is REACH?

REACH—Respond, Evaluate, Advocate, Connect and Heal—is an embedded social-services program within the Jeffersontown Police Department that supports victims of crime and individuals experiencing substance use, mental-health, homelessness and other complex needs.

The REACH Process

What the Name Means in Practice

The REACH model describes how program personnel move from first contact toward individualized support and connection.

1

Respond

REACH personnel meet people where they are when a need, crisis or referral is identified.

2

Evaluate

REACH personnel listen, assess needs and identify concerns, strengths and appropriate next steps.

3

Advocate

REACH helps individuals and families navigate systems, rights, services and barriers.

4

Connect

REACH connects people with mutually agreed-upon treatment, resources and community supports.

5

Heal

REACH provides continued support as individuals and families work toward safety, recovery, stability and long-term wellbeing.

Real Impact. Real Change.

Connection Can Change What Happens Next.

One early Angel Outreach case involved a young adult who had experienced repeated accidental drug poisonings and emergency-service calls. REACH personnel conducted four home visits, supported the family, provided harm-reduction education, accompanied the individual into treatment and connected the individual with additional recovery and vocational resources.

Program follow-up reported no new accidental drug poisonings or calls for service for three years after those services were provided.

How the Foundation Supports REACH

The City Funds the Team. Foundation Support Helps the Program Go Further.

The City of Jeffersontown funds REACH personnel—the professionals who respond, evaluate, advocate and connect. The Jeffersontown Police Foundation can help fill important gaps beyond salaries, providing additional resources and opportunities that strengthen the program's ability to serve the community.

Training is one of the program's biggest funding gaps. Foundation support can help provide specialized professional development in trauma, victim services, behavioral health, substance use, homelessness, deflection and other complex areas encountered by REACH personnel.

REACH History

Built in Jeffersontown

REACH grew from years of Jeffersontown Police Department innovation in police-assisted recovery, victim services and embedded social work.

2015

Alternative Response Begins

JPD began researching alternatives to drug-use arrests and officers began carrying naloxone.

2016

Kentucky's First Angel Program

JPD became the first Angel Program in Kentucky and the 100th partner of PAARI.

2020

Embedded Social Work

JPD's first embedded social worker helped launch Victim Services and expand civilian social-service support.

2022

REACH Launches

Victim Services, Angel Outreach and other social-service pathways came together under the REACH model.
