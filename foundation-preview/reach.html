Jeffersontown Police Department • REACH Unit

Help That Reaches Beyond the Call.

REACH is a social worker-led program embedded within the Jeffersontown Police Department, connecting victims of crime and people experiencing substance use, mental-health, homelessness and other crises with individualized support and a path forward.

Meeting People Where They Are

There isn't just one way to reach REACH.

People often need help before they know where to look for it. REACH creates multiple pathways to connect individuals and families with support—through police response, proactive outreach, community partnerships and self-referral.

Incident-Based Follow-Up

REACH reviews police reports and follows up when social-service needs are identified.

On-Scene Response

REACH personnel can respond alongside officers when specialized support is needed.

Officer Referral

Officers can connect community members directly with REACH for follow-up and assistance.

Community Referral

Community members and partner agencies can request REACH assistance.

Post-Overdose Outreach

Follow-up after non-fatal overdose connects individuals and families with treatment and recovery resources.

Targeted Outreach

Proactive engagement helps connect people with services before another crisis occurs.

Louisville Situation Table

Partner agencies coordinate rapid responses for individuals and families experiencing elevated risk.

Community Engagement

Education and outreach bring information, resources and connection directly into the community.

Self-Referral / Angel Program

Individuals can come directly to JPD and ask for help with substance use and treatment connection.

What REACH Means

Respond. Evaluate. Advocate. Connect. Heal.

REACH is more than an acronym. It describes how the team moves from first contact toward individualized support, connection and stability.

01

Respond

Meet people where they are when a need, crisis or referral is identified.

02

Evaluate

Listen, assess needs and develop an individualized plan with the person or family.

03

Advocate

Help navigate systems, rights, services and barriers that can make getting help difficult.

04

Connect

Link people with mutually agreed-upon treatment, resources and community support.

05

Heal

Support the next steps toward safety, recovery, stability and long-term wellbeing.

Who We Help

One team. Many kinds of need.

Crises rarely fit neatly into one category. REACH can combine services and tailor support around the individual and family.

Victims of Crime

Trauma-informed advocacy, safety planning, protective-order guidance, court accompaniment, victim rights, criminal-justice navigation and referrals.

Mental Health

Crisis support, safety planning, treatment connections, family support, placement assistance and help navigating behavioral-health services.

Substance Use & Recovery

Angel Program access, treatment connection, post-overdose outreach, recovery support, education and naloxone distribution.

Housing & Basic Needs

Assessment, community referrals, homelessness outreach, prevention, basic-needs support and connections designed to promote stability.

Beyond the Referral

Sometimes one barrier stands between crisis and stability.

REACH doesn't simply hand someone a phone number. The team works to identify what is preventing a person from accessing help and coordinates practical solutions whenever possible.

Transportation

Helping people reach treatment, appointments and other essential services when transportation is the barrier.

Basic Needs

Practical support and outreach supplies that help address immediate needs while longer-term solutions are developed.

Naloxone Supplies

Distribution bags, resource materials and other supplies that support community naloxone access and overdose prevention.

Connection

Working across agencies and systems so individuals and families do not have to navigate complex services alone.

What REACH Can Change

Connection can change what happens next.

From repeated crisis to sustained connection.

One of REACH's early Angel Outreach cases involved a young adult who had experienced repeated accidental drug poisonings and emergency-service calls. Two embedded social workers conducted four home visits, supported the family, provided harm-reduction education, accompanied the individual into treatment and connected them with additional recovery and vocational resources.

The program's follow-up reported no new accidental drug poisonings or calls for service for three years after those services were provided.

Built in Jeffersontown

A history of finding another way.

REACH grew from years of JPD innovation in police-assisted recovery, victim services and embedded social work.

2015

Alternative Response Begins

JPD began researching alternatives to drug-use arrests and officers began carrying naloxone.

2016

Kentucky's First Angel Program

JPD became the first Angel Program in Kentucky and the 100th partner of PAARI.

2020

Embedded Social Work

JPD's first embedded social worker helped launch Victim Services and expand civilian social-service support.

2022

REACH Launches

Victim Services, Angel Outreach and other social-service pathways came together under the REACH model.

Need Help?

Start with REACH.

If you or a loved one needs assistance after victimization, or is struggling with substance use, mental-health needs or another crisis, REACH can help navigate the next step. For emergencies, call 911.
